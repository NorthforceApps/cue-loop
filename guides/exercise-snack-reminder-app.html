Exercise snack reminder app — micro-workouts through the day

Exercise snacks are short bursts of movement — a minute or two of squats, stairs or stretching — spread through the day instead of saved for one workout. The moves are easy; remembering to do them is the hard part. An exercise snack reminder app solves exactly that: CueLoop sends a gentle cue on your own schedule, and you choose the move.

What counts as an exercise snack?

Anything short enough to do where you are, without changing clothes:

  • 10 squats or sit-to-stands from your chair.
  • Calf raises while the kettle boils.
  • Wall push-ups against a desk or kitchen counter.
  • One flight of stairs or a two-minute brisk walk.
  • Neck and shoulder rolls or a standing stretch after a long sit.

Turn each snack into a cue

  1. Add a cue loop and name it after the move, for example “Squats” or “Stairs”.
  2. Write the exact move in Message (optional) — “10 squats, slow on the way down” — so it appears with the cue in the notification panel.
  3. Set the interval: every 30–60 minutes suits most desk days.
  4. Choose active hours and days so cues stop in the evening and at weekends if you like.
  5. Pick a mode: vibration or a screen flash keeps it private in an office; a soft sound works at home.
  6. Try it with the test button (for example “Test vibration”), then start it.

An example day

  • Every 45 minutes, 9:00–17:00 — 10 squats.
  • Every 60 minutes — one flight of stairs or a short walk.
  • Every 30 minutes — neck and shoulder rolls.

On the free plan one cue runs at a time, which is enough to start: keep a single “Exercise snack” cue and change its message each week — a squat week, then a push-up week, then a stairs week. To run several snack cues side by side, use your weekly free day or CueLoop Pro.

See which snacks you actually did

Switch on Habit reflection for a cue and each time it fires you can tap Done or Not done straight from the notification. The Reflection screen — the calendar icon on the home screen — shows each day and week at a glance. There are no streaks to lose and no account: the record stays on your phone.

Keep it small enough to do

A snack only works if you do it when the cue arrives. If you start skipping, make the move smaller or the interval longer rather than pushing harder. If you have an injury or a health condition, check with a professional before adding new exercises.

A reminder, not a workout plan

CueLoop does not come with an exercise library or training programs. It is the reliable, well-timed cue — on your phone and, if you use one, your Wear OS watch — that you pair with whichever moves suit you.

Frequently asked questions

What are exercise snacks?

Short bursts of movement — a minute or two of squats, stairs, wall push-ups or stretching — spread through the day instead of done as one workout.

How often should a snack cue come?

Many people use every 30–60 minutes during the working day. Pick an interval you will actually follow; you can change it at any time.

Can I see which snacks I did?

Yes. Turn on Habit reflection for the cue and tap Done or Not done from the notification; the Reflection screen shows your day and week, stored only on your phone.

Is CueLoop a workout app?

No. It has no exercise library or training plans. It gives you the well-timed cue, and you choose the moves.
