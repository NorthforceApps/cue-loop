ADHD reminder app — gentle nudges for time blindness

When time slips away, steady nudges help more than long planners. A simple ADHD reminder app can anchor the day with recurring cues you can't easily ignore. CueLoop gives you gentle, repeating reminders — a sound, a buzz or a screen flash on your phone and Wear OS watch — to ease time blindness and keep you moving.

How recurring cues help with time blindness

Time blindness makes hours disappear: you sit down for "a few minutes" and look up much later. Recurring cues break that by marking the passage of time. A nudge every 20–30 minutes is a low-pressure checkpoint — drink water, switch tasks, stand up, or just notice where the time went — without needing to watch a clock.

Low-friction by design

Setup is fast on purpose: wheel pickers, an instant Test button and a start-now option mean a working cue in seconds. There's no long onboarding, no elaborate routine to build, and no account to create. If a tool takes effort to start, it doesn't get used — so CueLoop keeps the runway short.

Set up an anchor cue

  1. Add a cue loop named for what you need — "Check the time", "Switch task", "Water".
  2. Set a 20–30 minute interval and active hours that match your day.
  3. Pick a mode: a wrist buzz is great for ADHD because it's physical and hard to tune out.
  4. Run a few in parallel — a movement nudge, a hydration nudge, a focus check — each on its own rhythm.

No streaks, no guilt

Many habit and ADHD apps lean on streaks, points and social feeds, which can backfire when a missed day feels like failure. CueLoop has none of that — just a gentle, reliable cue when you asked for one. Miss it? Nothing breaks; the next cue still comes.

Bring it to your wrist

Cues mirror to a Wear OS watch as a vibration, which is easy to feel and hard to ignore even when the phone is away. See the Wear OS reminder guide, or try a focus rhythm with the gentle Pomodoro alternative.
